# India Digital Spending & Tax Health Monitor

## Key Findings (2018--2024)

This analysis compares state-wise UPI transaction amount growth with GST
return filing growth to understand where digital payment activity and
tax filing trends move differently.

### Finding 1: Smaller states show more frequent divergence

Mizoram recorded the highest number of flagged quarters, with 16 out of
24 scored quarters, followed by Jammu and Kashmir (15), Lakshadweep
(13), Manipur (13), and Sikkim (12).

Interestingly, the major UPI payment states do not appear among the six
states with the most frequent flags.

This suggests that differences between UPI growth and GST filing growth
occur more frequently in smaller states. One possible explanation is
that smaller transaction bases can produce larger percentage changes
from relatively small movements. However, this explanation has not been
independently tested.

### Finding 2: States show different directions of divergence

The direction of divergence is not consistent across all states.

-   Mizoram and Sikkim recorded GST growth ahead of UPI growth in every
    flagged quarter (16/16 and 12/12 respectively).
-   Andaman and Nicobar Islands showed the opposite pattern in all nine
    flagged quarters, with UPI growth ahead of GST growth.
-   Jammu and Kashmir and Manipur experienced divergence in both
    directions, rather than consistently favouring one side.

These patterns suggest that some states have a more consistent direction
of divergence, while others experience changing differences between the
two indicators. The consistency alone does not establish an underlying
cause.

### Finding 3: Extreme divergence is concentrated in Jammu and Kashmir and Manipur

The five highest divergence scores all came from Jammu and Kashmir or
Manipur.

During these quarters, UPI amount growth ranged from approximately 144%
to 697%, while GST filing growth ranged from -27% to 4%.

Two particularly extreme observations were:

-   Jammu and Kashmir, 2020 Q4, following a large UPI decline in 2019
    Q4.
-   Manipur, 2024 Q2, following a large UPI decline in 2023 Q2.

These comparisons suggest that unusually low previous-year values can
make subsequent YoY growth appear exceptionally high.

Therefore, extreme divergence scores should be treated as signals for
further investigation, rather than evidence of incorrect reporting or
unusual economic behaviour.

The relationship observed here is an association, not causation.

### Finding 4: The leading UPI states remained similar, but their rankings changed

The leading states by UPI amount share remained relatively stable
between 2018 Q1 and 2024 Q4, although their positions changed.

  State           2018 Q1         2024 Q4
  --------------- --------------- ---------------
  Maharashtra     12.64%          11.53%
  Karnataka       11.18%          11.53%
  Uttar Pradesh   8.11%           Outside top 3
  Telangana       Outside top 3   10.72%

Maharashtra remained among the leading states, while Karnataka increased
its relative share. Telangana entered the top three by 2024 Q4,
replacing Uttar Pradesh.

This indicates that the leading contributors remained broadly similar,
but their relative positions evolved over the analysis period.

### Finding 5: Real UPI growth declined over time, while large quarterly drops were limited to a few states

Median real UPI amount growth declined across the observed years:

  Year     Median real growth (percentage points)
  ------ ----------------------------------------
  2019                                        241
  2020                                        125
  2021                                        123
  2022                                         89
  2023                                         47
  2024                                         32

The early years show particularly high growth, partly because UPI
activity was starting from a smaller base. The 2020 and 2021
calculations also contain fewer scored quarters because of missing CPI
observations.

Separately, only six state-quarter observations recorded a
quarter-on-quarter UPI amount decline greater than 30%:

  State               Quarter     Change
  ------------------- --------- --------
  Chandigarh          2023 Q1       -54%
  Goa                 2020 Q2       -36%
  Jammu and Kashmir   2019 Q3       -39%
  Jammu and Kashmir   2019 Q4       -48%
  Manipur             2023 Q1       -45%
  Manipur             2023 Q2       -48%

These declines were concentrated in a small number of states and
quarters. The available datasets identify when and where the drops
occurred, but do not establish why they happened.

## Limits and Interpretation

The findings should be interpreted with the following limitations:

1.  **Association, not causation:** Differences between UPI growth and
    GST filing growth do not establish that one causes the other.
2.  **GST measures filing counts:** The dataset tracks GST returns
    filed, not the monetary value of GST collections or taxable
    transactions.
3.  **Different geographical attribution:** GST registration state does
    not necessarily represent the state where the underlying spending
    occurred.
4.  **UPI coverage:** PhonePe represents approximately 45% of UPI
    transaction volume, so its data is not a complete representation of
    all UPI activity.
5.  **Small-state volatility:** States with smaller transaction bases
    can experience unstable YoY growth rates, particularly when the
    previous-year comparison value is unusually low.
6.  **Missing CPI observations:** Missing inflation data prevents
    real-growth calculations for some state-quarter observations.

These findings are intended to identify patterns and areas for further
investigation, not to establish definitive economic explanations.
